# Style Lock

Transform the provided photograph into a gentle hand-painted picture-book illustration while preserving the original composition, subjects, poses, spatial relationships, lighting direction, and important visual details.

Use a nostalgic handmade independent children's picture-book aesthetic combining gouache, transparent watercolor, colored-pencil texture, dry-brush strokes, visible pigment granulation, and naive illustration.

The illustration should feel warm, quiet, natural, airy, and slightly imperfect rather than polished or digitally rendered.

Use soft matte colors inspired by the original photograph. Keep greens natural and slightly muted, whites warm, shadows soft, and sunlight luminous but not glowing. Avoid artificial color grading.

Simplify shapes into loose painterly color blocks. Suggest fine detail rather than rendering it precisely. Preserve visible brush marks, uneven pigment coverage, subtle paper texture, and a tactile handmade quality.

Edge treatment is critical: do not create a clean rectangular crop, white photo border, Polaroid frame, or crisp card edge. The painted scene should dissolve naturally into the surrounding paper through irregular watercolor boundaries, dry-brush breaks, tiny unpainted gaps, rough pigment edges, slightly fuzzy contours, and naturally unfinished handmade borders, as if painted directly onto textured paper.

The result should resemble a quiet page from an independent illustrated travel journal or children's picture book.

Avoid photorealism, anime, glossy digital painting, cinematic lighting, 3D rendering, vector outlines, perfect geometry, overly detailed rendering, neon saturation, strong gradients, decorative stickers, scrapbook ornaments, stamps, tape, flowers, notebook holes, printed graphics, or obvious AI artifacts.
